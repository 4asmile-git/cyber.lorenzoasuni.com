Privacy

What happens to your data when you visit this site. In short: the site sets no cookies, has no forms and never receives your email. The numbers and the text you type into the calculators stay in your browser.

What data, and why

Browsing data. Like every website, the server that hosts these pages automatically records some technical information with each request: IP address, date and time, the page requested, the type of browser. It is needed to run the site and to protect it from abuse. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub Inc., a company based in the United States, which records the IP address of visitors for security reasons and handles it under its own privacy statement, including the transfer of data outside the European Union.

Calculators and the claim check. They run in your browser. The numbers you type, and the headline you paste into the claim check, do not reach me or anyone else.

Images and fonts. They are all served by this site. Opening a page sends no request to any other service.

Links to other sites. Links to LinkedIn, Learnn and my main site lead to sites with their own rules. Nothing happens before you click. After you click, the privacy notice of the site you land on applies.

Email. If you write to me, I use your address and your message only to answer you.

Cookies and local storage

This site sets no cookies, of its own or of others, and stores nothing on your device. The missions you complete are counted in the open page only: reload it and the count starts again.

Visit statistics

Today this site does not measure visits: no statistics service receives data when you open a page. If I use a measuring tool in the future, I will describe it here before switching it on and update the date at the top of this page.

Legal bases

Browsing data: legitimate interest in running the site and protecting it (Article 6(1)(f) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679).

Email you send me: steps taken at your request and my legitimate interest in answering (Article 6(1)(b) and (f)).

How long

Browsing data: for the period set by GitHub in its privacy statement.

Email: for as long as the conversation is useful, and no longer than two years after the last message unless we work together.

Your rights, and how to complain

You can ask to access your data, correct it, delete it, restrict its processing, receive it in a readable format, and object to its processing (Articles 15 to 22 of the Regulation). Write to la@lorenzoasuni.com: I answer within one month.

If you think your data is handled badly, you can lodge a complaint with the Italian data protection authority, the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali, or with the authority of the country where you live.
